In EE 211, Basic Circuit Analysis, I completed a solar panel integration study focused on improving renewable energy use at schools. I compared rooftop and ground level solar strategies and considered how sunlight exposure, available space, and building features affect system placement. The study used C Building at President William McKinley High School as a case study, with the goal of identifying a practical configuration that could support classroom air conditioning and technology while reducing demand on the electrical grid.

My work centered on modeling and evaluating a rooftop solar layout in Aladdin. I recreated a simplified version of C Building and retained the roof features that mattered for placing the panels. The rooftop HVAC equipment made the geometry more complex, so I arranged the panels in four sections on the left, right, front, and back of the roof to use the available space. I then ran a yearly energy simulation in Aladdin. The final arrangement produced an estimated 154793 kilowatt hours of solar energy each year in the model.

This project taught me that an electrical design must account for physical constraints as well as energy goals. I learned how building shape, roof equipment, panel placement, and local weather data can affect a solar system before installation begins. It also strengthened my ability to turn engineering requirements into a visual model, evaluate simulation results, and explain a renewable energy proposal in a clear technical poster. The experience showed me how circuit analysis and energy modeling can contribute to lower operating costs and more resilient school facilities.
